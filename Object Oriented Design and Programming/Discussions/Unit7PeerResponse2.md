Progressive delay (as Kaona suggested) solves the "attacker deliberately locks out a real user" problem, since the account never actually becomes fully inaccessible, just slower to attempt. But delay alone is fairly weak against a distributed brute force spread across many IPs hitting many usernames at low frequency each. Scoping some of the limiting per IP address as well, alongside the per-username delay, would catch that case without reintroducing the lockout-as-attack problem.
