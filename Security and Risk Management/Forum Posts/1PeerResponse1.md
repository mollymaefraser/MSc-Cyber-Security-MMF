## Peer Response

Hi Elvira,

Thank you for your insightful post. You’ve raised a critical point regarding how datasets can reinforce existing inequalities. I particularly appreciated your reference to Vicente (2023); the idea that humans can "inherit" AI biases creates a dangerous feedback loop where technological errors become cemented as human "truth" in human rights contexts.

To build on your point about the lack of accountability and the "black box" nature of AI (Chikwarti and Wong, 2025), I would like to suggest a few specific measures that could have prevented the risks you highlighted:

### Preventive Measures for Fairness and Accountability

* Algorithmic Impact Assessments (AIAs): Before deploying technology in a human rights investigation, organisations should mandate AIAs. Unlike a standard audit, an AIA is a proactive tool used to evaluate the potential effects of a system on fundamental rights and marginalised groups (Metcalf et al., 2021). This could prevent the "unfair conclusions" you mentioned by identifying disparate impacts before they occur.
* Diverse Data Provenance and Representative Sampling: To prevent the bias noted by Vicente (2023), investigators must move beyond "convenience data." For example, if facial recognition is used to identify victims or perpetrators, the training sets must be rigorously balanced. Research has shown that some commercial AI systems have error rates of up to 34.7% for darker-skinned females compared to just 0.8% for lighter-skinned males (Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018). Ensuring high-quality, representative data is a technical necessity, not just a moral one.
* Explainable AI (XAI) Frameworks: To counter the "black box" issue, developers should utilise XAI techniques. These frameworks provide a "right to explanation" for data subjects, ensuring that if a technology "obscures the identity of a violator" (Land and Aronson, 2020), there is a traceable logic path that human investigators can audit to reconstruct the event.

By integrating these measures during the design phase, we move from a reactive "mistake-fixing" culture to a "human rights by design" approach.
